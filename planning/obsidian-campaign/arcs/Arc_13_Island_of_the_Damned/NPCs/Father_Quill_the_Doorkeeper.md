# Father Quill the Doorkeeper

#custom #campaign #npc

> The last priest of the Nameless Abbey, who has held the chapel door against the dead
> for nineteen years by never once leaving and never once sleeping more than two hours.
> He is gaunt to translucence, funny in the way of people far past fear, and he has
> personally laid to rest more souls than live in Prontera. He has been waiting, he
> says, for relief. He doesn't mean reinforcements. He means *rest.*

- **Role:** **ally / quest-giver and mentor** — the party's guide through an island where
  the dead won't stay down, and the conscience that makes the necromancy thread *personal.*
- **Location:** the Nameless Abbey chapel; the cursed grounds he patrols.
- **Propp function (donor):** provides the consecrated routes, the rites of laying-to-rest,
  and the names of the dead — including, perhaps, ones the party knew.

## What he wants
To finish. Quill has held the line so long he's forgotten what for; he wants the party to
either help him *win* (close the boundary-wound feeding the Abbey) or, failing that, to
witness him so his nineteen-year vigil meant something. He is the campaign's portrait of
duty stretched past the point of reward.

## What he knows
- The dead won't rest because the **boundary between worlds is failing** here (the same
  dissolution the party crossed in Arc 12, surfacing in the realm of the dead).
- That the unrest is *organized* — **Beelzebub** isn't haunting the Abbey, he's *hosting*
  it: the demon lords are convening, the coalition from Satan Morroc's notes gone active.
- The names and faces of the risen — and that some of them are souls the party *failed to
  lay to rest* in Acts I–III (Payon, the desert, Niflheim's overflow upstream).

## Dialogue hooks (three doors)
1. *Reverence:* "Tell us how to lay them down, Father — all of them." → he gives the full
   rites and routes and fights beside the party to the last; best outcome, and his vigil
   ends in victory rather than collapse.
2. *Pragmatic:* "Just get us to whatever's organizing this." → he points them at Beelzebub's
   sanctum but won't abandon his door; the party reach the council faster but leave souls
   unrested behind them.
3. *Callous:* "They're just monsters, Father. Move." → he looks at them a long moment —
   "That's what the necromancers say too" — and the party have lost the one man who could
   have named their own dead to them. (See the side quest.)

## Theme tie-in
Quill is **the necromancy thread's moral inverse** — Voss (Arc 2) and the coalition *use*
the dead; Quill has spent his life *freeing* them, at the cost of his own. He's the
campaign's hardest "what are you becoming?": not a warning of corruption but a model of a
goodness so total it's indistinguishable from self-erasure. Is *that* a road the party
should walk? The campaign genuinely isn't sure, and neither is he.

## GM notes / secrets
- Quill is the delivery mechanism for the Arc-2 / Niflheim necromancy payoff and the
  "lay a known dead to rest" side quest. Keep him central.
- His exhaustion is the point — if the party "win" without relieving him, he simply *stops,*
  mid-sentence, his vigil over. A quiet, devastating beat the GM can hold for the right moment.
