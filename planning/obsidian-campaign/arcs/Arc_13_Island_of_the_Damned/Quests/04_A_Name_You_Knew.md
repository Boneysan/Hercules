# Quest — A Name You Knew

#custom #campaign #quest

> Among the thousands of unrested dead on the island, Father Quill has found one the
> party will recognize — someone they met, or failed, or laid to rest badly, long ago.
> The dead remember being known. This one has been waiting for the people whose faces
> it can still almost recall.

- **Type:** side — the necromancy thread made personal; a Payon/Act-I callback
- **Level band:** 125–155
- **Quest-giver:** [[../NPCs/Father_Quill_the_Doorkeeper]]
- **Key NPC:** a returning Act-I dead (GM's choice — see below)

## Hook (Separation)
Quill keeps a register of the risen, and one name stops the party cold. The boundary's
failure has pulled a specific soul here — someone from the party's own past: a grave they
robbed or honored in **Payon** (Arc 2), a king's servant from the **desert** (Arc 3), a
refugee who didn't survive the **sea-crossing** (Arc 5), or an **ally the party lost** in
Act II (Wynne, Mara, Sabine). The threshold is memory: to help, the party must walk back
into a choice they thought was finished.

## Spaces
- **Initiation:** the Abbey's outer grounds, where the personally-connected dead drift apart
  from the anonymous host.
- **Reveal space:** a quiet grave-niche where the known soul waits — lucid enough to *almost*
  remember the party, which is worse than not remembering at all.

## Challenge (conditions → consequences)
- **The dead remember the party's record:** how this soul greets them depends on Acts I–III
  — a grave honored (Arc 2's comb returned) meets them with peace; a grave looted or a soul
  burned meets them with grief or fury. The Catechism/laying-to-rest tally is *named and faced.*
- **Lay it to rest properly (Quill's rite):** requires the party to *acknowledge* what they
  did or failed to do — a roleplay reckoning, not a damage check. Done right, the soul rests
  and *blesses* the party (a boon for the main quest's boss); done callously, it joins
  Beelzebub's host against them.
- **Some debts can't be paid, only owned:** if the soul is a lost ally, there may be no
  "fixing" it — only sitting with it. The quest deliberately offers no clean absolution.

## Quest item / Object (symbolic)
**The Almost-Remembered Token** — a small possession of the dead the party once handled
(Arc 2's funerary comb, Arc 1's child-drawing, an ally's keepsake like Wynne's ledger-page).
The soul gives it *back,* having carried it through death — returning to the party the exact
object that once measured their choice. The campaign's relics come full circle: the things
the party carried as evidence of who they were are handed back by the dead as a verdict.

## Choices & branches (a price)
- **Reckon honestly and lay them to rest** → the soul rests, blesses the party (main-quest
  boon), and the party carry a closed grief instead of an open wound into Act IV. Costs them
  the comfort of pretending old choices didn't matter.
- **Refuse to face it / put it down like a monster** → fast, and the party stay armored — but
  the soul joins the council's host, and its face *returns* in Arc 18 (Niflheim, where
  Himmelmez rules exactly such unrested dead) to accuse them. Deferral with compound interest.

## Reward (Return)
- **Mechanical:** a main-quest blessing (or a harder fight); the returned token; modest EXP.
- **Narrative:** the campaign's necromancy thread stops being abstract — the party reckon
  with *their own* dead before facing the witch who rules them (Arc 18).

## Throughline
- **Plants:** the reckoning → Arc 18 (Himmelmez's domain of unrested dead; this soul may reappear).
- **Pays off:** the specific Act I–II choice tied to this dead; the laying-to-rest tally.
- **"What are you becoming?":** the dead hold up the party's oldest choices, by name, and ask if they've grown or just gotten stronger.

## GM scripting notes
- Pick the returning dead for *maximum personal weight* — ideally an ally the party lost, or a
  grave they handled badly. Tailor it to the table's actual history.
- This is a roleplay reckoning; never let it be "solved" by damage. It's the emotional rehearsal
  for Arc 18's "face the dead you failed."
