# Quest — Underground Prison Cleanup

#custom #campaign #quest

> Beneath the banquet's gold is a prison full of people the kingdom preferred not to remember.

- **Type:** side (dungeon investigation and moral price)
- **Level band:** 180-205
- **Quest-giver:** [[../NPCs/Royal_Guard_Commander]]
- **Key NPCs:** [[../NPCs/Kronecker_G_Heine]], political prisoners, cult jailers
- **Frames:** Prontera Underground Prison as both leveling route and buried civic guilt.

## Spaces
- **Threshold / Separation:** the stair behind the royal chapel, opened with a command seal.
- **Dream-landscape / Initiation:** prison corridors where every locked cell asks whether security and justice are the same thing.
- **Climax space:** the old execution cistern, now used as Bijou's ritual staging room.

## Quest object
**The Prison Ledger** — a list of prisoners, charges, and blank spaces where names were removed. It can condemn the court or coordinate an evacuation.

## Challenge (condition -> consequence)
- Freeing prisoners before clearing cult jailers creates chaos but may save innocents from execution.
- Clearing jailers first keeps order but gives collaborators time to destroy records.
- Reading the ledger aloud to Kronecker unlocks reform support; handing it to Elric unlocks military efficiency.

## Branching choice
1. **Noble:** free the unjustly held -> more allies and witnesses, slower dungeon progress.
2. **Pragmatic:** secure the prison infrastructure -> cleaner military outcome, fewer personal rescues.
3. **Dark:** trade prisoner names for noble leverage -> powerful evidence, permanent Catechism tally pressure.

## Reward
- **Mechanical:** safe prison route, prison witness allies, evidence against Bijou's faction.
- **Narrative:** the party decide whether saving the kingdom includes judging it.

## Throughline
- **Plants:** named witnesses who can stand in Arc 19's ally turnout.
- **Pays off:** Act II's institutional rot; Prontera is not exempt.
- **"What are you becoming?":** jailers with cleaner keys, liberators with bloodier halls, or politicians with better files?
