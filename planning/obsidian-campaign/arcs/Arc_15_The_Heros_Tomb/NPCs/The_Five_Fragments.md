# The Five Fragments — Cowardice · Hatred · Malice · Despair · Agony

#custom #campaign #npc

> Not five monsters. Five *pieces of one man.* When the demon broke Thanatos apart from
> the inside, it broke him along these five fault-lines, and each became a guardian of
> his tomb — and a mirror. The party do not fight strangers in this tower. They fight
> the worst of what they themselves have been becoming for fourteen arcs, wearing a dead
> hero's face.

- **Role:** the arc's **antagonist** — but there is no human villain here. The Fragments are
  *embodied trials,* each a floor, each judging the party against their own record.
- **Location:** Thanatos Tower F9–12 and the summit; one Fragment per stage.
- **Propp function:** the villain who **curses** (each Fragment afflicts the party with the
  flaw it embodies) — and, uniquely, who **reflects** (it fights *as* the party, scaled to
  the Catechism/Doppelganger tally from Arcs 4–14).

## The Fragments as mirrors
Each Fragment is keyed to a thread the campaign has tracked. The GM tunes each one to the
party's actual history:

- **Cowardice** — the choices the party made out of *fear* (Holt's sin, Arc 1). Fights by
  exploiting their hesitations; weakest against a party that acted bravely when it cost them.
- **Hatred** — every enemy the party killed *because it was easy and they were sure* (the
  Martyrs' Crypt, Arc 8). Fights with the party's own most brutal tactics.
- **Malice** — the cruelties of convenience: the looted dead, Echo returned for bounty, the
  sedated miners. Fights dirty, the way the party did when no one was watching.
- **Despair** — the moments the party stopped believing they could win (Act III's "we are
  losing"). Drains resolve; strongest against a party that gave up on saving everyone.
- **Agony** — what the party *carried* and never set down: the named dead, the lost allies,
  the chalk crossed off the Tally-Board (Arc 14). The final guardian; it fights with the
  party's own grief.

## How they "talk" (three doors, per Fragment)
The Fragments don't negotiate — they *accuse,* and the party answer with deeds. Per the
campaign's dialogue rule, each confrontation offers three responses:
1. *Own it:* acknowledge the flaw → the Fragment *weakens* (a mirror loses power when looked at
   honestly); the hardest and most rewarding path.
2. *Deny it:* insist they're the good ones → the Fragment *strengthens* (denial is its food —
   the Glast Heim lesson, Arc 8, made mechanical).
3. *Embrace it:* lean into the flaw for power → the party win the floor faster and the
   **Catechism tally spikes,** loading the Arc 19 mirror and narrowing their endings.

## Theme tie-in
The Five Fragments are the **campaign's entire "what are you becoming?" question, assembled
into a boss.** Thanatos won and was broken into exactly these five pieces; the tower asks
whether the party are already carrying the same five fractures. It is the most literal the
theme ever gets — and the dress rehearsal for Arc 19, where the party stand where Thanatos
stood and decide whether to break the same way.

## GM notes / secrets
- **Tune each Fragment to the party's real record.** Pull from the running Catechism tally and
  the named choices of Arcs 1–14. A party that walked clean faces weaker Fragments; a party
  trailing compromise faces near-mirror versions of themselves.
- The *own-it* path should be both the hardest and the only one that leaves the party *lighter*
  going into the finale. Reward honesty mechanically.
- These are guardians of the **Memory of Thanatos** (the MVP) — the Fragments must be faced to
  reach him, and how the party answered them changes how the Memory receives them.
