# Quest — The Five Doors of a Dead Hero

#custom #campaign #quest

> Climb a tower that is really a corpse. Each floor is a piece of the hero who saved
> the world by sealing the demon inside himself — and was eaten alive from within for
> his trouble. At the top is what's left of him, and the whole terrible story of what
> "winning" costs. You will need it. You're about to be asked the same question.

- **Type:** main (arc climax; the campaign's moral center)
- **Level band:** 180–220
- **Quest-giver:** [[../NPCs/Keeper_Lysandra]]
- **Key NPCs:** [[../NPCs/The_Five_Fragments]]; [[Characters/Mortals/Thanatos]]
- **MVP:** **Memory of Thanatos**

## Hook (Separation)
The Morroc seal (Arc 3's shaft) is failing now, and Lysandra reveals why the party must climb
*this* tower first: the man who last contained what's under Morroc left his confession here,
written in five floors of his own broken soul. The threshold is the Nightmare Clock Tower —
time itself unravelling as the boundary fails — then the crossing to Thanatos Tower, where the
party climb *into a dead hero's psyche.*

## Spaces
- **Threshold:** the Nightmare Clock Tower 3F — warped time; the party climb *out of* the
  ordinary world's clock into a place where one moment (Thanatos's last) never ended.
- **Initiation:** **Thanatos Tower F9–12** — a vertical dissection of one soul; each floor a
  Fragment (Cowardice → Agony). Ascending is walking *through* the hero, and through the
  party's own accumulated flaws (see [[../NPCs/The_Five_Fragments]]).
- **Climax:** the summit — the **Memory of Thanatos,** the hero as the thing he beat.

## Challenge (conditions → consequences)
- **Five mirror-fights:** each Fragment is tuned to the party's actual record (the Catechism
  tally) and answered by *owning, denying, or embracing* the flaw it names. Owning weakens
  them and leaves the party lighter; denying/embracing feeds the finale's mirror.
- **Lysandra's rite:** with her *humility* door, the party reach the Memory able to *speak*
  with it — receiving the full revelation; without it, the Memory is only a fight, and the
  story stays half-told.
- **The Memory is not an enemy to defeat but a confession to survive:** the fight ends not when
  Thanatos's Memory is destroyed but when the party *understand* it — a boss resolved by
  comprehension, the Kiel D-01 lesson (Arc 10) at maximum weight.

## Quest item / Object (symbolic)
**Thanatos's Last Word** — the hero's final testament, assembled from fragments gathered on
each floor: *"I thought a hero had to carry it alone. That was the demon's first lie."* It is
the single most important object in the campaign — the key that makes the Arc 19 Choice
*winnable,* because it reveals that Thanatos's fatal error was not the sealing but the
**solitude.** The party hold, in one line, the difference between his doom and their chance.
(Pair it with the Arc 4 Pilgrim's Catechism: the two books are the finale's two paths.)

## Choices & branches (a price)
- **Receive the Memory with honesty (own the Fragments)** → the party descend the tower
  *lighter,* carrying Thanatos's warning instead of repeating his fracture; the Arc 19 Choice
  opens its full range of endings, including the ones he never had.
- **Conquer the tower by force (deny/embrace)** → the party win, gain power, and arrive at the
  finale exactly as Thanatos did — strong, certain, and alone, with the demon's "first lie"
  still believed. Some Arc 19 endings quietly close.

## Reward (Return)
- **Mechanical:** Memory of Thanatos resolved; Thanatos Tower F9–12 leveling to ~220; Thanatos's
  Last Word; Thanatos Daily Quest EXP (Fragments turn-in).
- **Narrative:** the campaign's whole backstory and moral center delivered — the party now
  *understand* the Morroc threat and the price of winning, and recognize they are one arc from
  the same dilemma.

## Throughline
- **Plants:** Thanatos's "you don't have to carry it alone" → the Arc 19 Choice's winnable path.
- **Pays off:** the "sleeping demon" (Arc 3) full backstory; the false-hero mirror (Arc 10); the
  Catechism tally (Arc 4) judged floor by floor; "certainty is the curse" (Arc 8).
- **"What are you becoming?":** Thanatos won and it unmade him — the party measure themselves
  against his five fractures and decide whether they're already cracking the same way.
