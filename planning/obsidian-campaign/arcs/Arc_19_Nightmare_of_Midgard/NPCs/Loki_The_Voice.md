# Loki — "The Voice" (campaign treatment)

#custom #campaign #god

> See canon: [[Characters/Gods/Loki]]. This file is his role as the campaign's hidden
> architect, revealed at last in the finale.

> Since Arc 1 there has been a Voice — Holt's certainty, Sabra's mercy, the manifest's
> "voice that pays in certainty," a dying Valkyrie's laugh, an empty chair at the
> demons' council. The Whisperers thought they served a cause. The demons thought they
> coordinated themselves. Everyone was an instrument. The hand on all of them was Loki,
> the agent of Ragnarok — and the joke, the one he finds genuinely funny, is that the
> end of the world never needed an army. It only needed people doing reasonable things
> for reasons they chose themselves.

- **Role:** the campaign's **true architect** — unseen through Acts I–III, revealed in Arc 19. Not a boss to out-damage but a *truth* to confront.
- **Location:** the Ash Vacuum / Dimensional Gap; everywhere the cascade has touched, in
  retrospect.
- **Propp function:** the villain who has done *every* function at one remove — through
  others. He never attacked, stole, trapped, or cursed the party directly. He arranged for
  them to do it to themselves and each other.

## What he wants
Ragnarok — not from malice, but because he is the part of the world's story that *ends* it,
and he finds the ending beautiful, or at least *interesting.* Loki doesn't hate the party. He
*likes* them. They've been the best instrument of all: a party that grew strong fighting
monsters, exactly as he needed, never noticing that *becoming strong was the plan.*

## What he knows / reveals
- That the Whisperers were **archaeologists, not authors** (Arc 3's capstone) — the method to
  weaken seals was always there; Loki just pointed people at it.
- That every villain the party met was *sincere* — fear, curiosity, mercy, grievance, profit,
  zeal, duty, conquest. Loki needed no converts, only *reasonable people.* That's the horror
  he unveils: the apocalypse was crowd-sourced.
- That the party's own road — the Catechism, the compromises, the power — has made them *his*
  most perfect tool, and that Thanatos's "you don't have to carry it alone" is the one thing
  he couldn't account for.

## Dialogue hooks (the finale's confrontation)
Loki does not bargain like a demon or recruit like Himmelmez. He *explains,* and invites the
party to find it as funny as he does:
1. *Defiance:* "We'll stop you." → "You've been *helping* me. Marvelously. Don't stop on my account."
2. *Despair:* "Then it was all pointless." → "Pointless? You *grew.* That was never pointless.
   It just wasn't *yours.*" — the campaign's darkest beat.
3. *The turn:* the party use what Thanatos taught (Arc 15) and what Echo proved (Arc 10) — that
   the road has an *off-ramp,* that a thing can refuse what it was made to become — and Loki,
   for the first time, stops finding it funny. This is the only "attack" that lands on a god of
   endings: a story that declines to end the way it was written.

## Theme tie-in
Loki is **"what are you becoming?" revealed as "what were you *made* to become?"** — and the
finale's whole moral weight is the party answering: *not this. Not because we're told the
story ends here.* He is the reason the campaign's question was never rhetorical; he's been
scoring the answer the whole time.

## GM notes / secrets
- **Keep him entirely off-page until here.** Every Voice seed (Arcs 1, 3, 11, 13) pays off in
  this reveal; if the players assembled them, let *them* name Loki before the GM does.
- Loki is **not defeated by damage.** Frame the finale so the party's accumulated choices —
  Thanatos's lesson, Echo saved, the New World alliance, the grief faced, the Mirror-Shard —
  are what unmake his certainty. He loses when the party's story stops being his.
- His affection for the party is real and unsettling — play him delighted, not hateful. The
  most chilling thing he can do is *congratulate* them.
