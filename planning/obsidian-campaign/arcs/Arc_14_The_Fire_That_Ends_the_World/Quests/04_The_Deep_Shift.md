# Quest — The Deep Shift

#custom #campaign #quest

> There are still crews on the deep shift when the mountain starts to wake — men the
> church told to keep digging. The party can get them out, but the galleries are
> collapsing in heat-blooms, the routes are flooding with fire, and there is not time
> for everyone. Choose fast. The mountain won't wait while you agonize.

- **Type:** side — a race-against-the-clock evacuation with a who-do-you-save fork
- **Level band:** 130–160
- **Quest-giver:** [[../NPCs/Foreman_Dunmar]]
- **Key NPCs:** [[../NPCs/Prelate_Hesma]] (whose suppression put them down there)

## Hook (Separation)
Hesma kept the deep shift working to prove the tremors were nothing; now the tremors are
something, and the crews are trapped below. Dunmar can't reach them alone. The threshold is a
literal descent into a collapsing mine against the clock — the campaign's "we are losing" act
distilled to a stopwatch and a list of names.

## Spaces
- **Initiation:** the deep galleries — narrowing, heating, the rock breathing; a dungeon that
  is actively *dying* around the party.
- **Climax (soft):** the lowest gallery overlooking the magma cathedral — where the party
  glimpse Ifrit's chamber (telegraphing the main quest) while dragging out the last crew.

## Challenge (conditions → consequences)
- **Triage under a timer:** multiple crews, branching flooded routes, not enough time for a
  clean sweep. The party *must* prioritize — the strong who can move fast, or the injured who
  can't; the near crew or the larger far one. There is **no route that saves everyone,** and
  the quest refuses to pretend otherwise.
- **Dunmar's maps vs. blind:** with his *solidarity* door, the party know the shortcuts and
  save more; without, every wrong turn costs a crew.
- **Hesma's records:** finding the suppressed survey data *in the temple office above* would
  have given hours of warning — the party can carry that knowledge into the confrontation with
  her as proof she chose this.

## Quest item / Object (symbolic)
**The Shift Tally-Board** — the foreman's roster of who went down, names chalked beside lamp
numbers. By the end the party will have crossed some names out themselves. It is the
campaign's most direct descendant of **Wynne's ledger** (Arc 1) — the book of names with some
crossed out — now wielded by the party's own hand under fire. The object that opened the
campaign (a list of adventurers) returns as a list of the saved and the lost, written in the
party's chalk. *This is what becoming strong has made them: the ones who decide which names
stay.*

## Choices & branches (the quest IS the fork)
- The triage choices are the quest. Who the party save (and who they write off) is recorded —
  survivors become grateful Veins voices for Act IV; the lost are *named,* and may surface in
  Arc 18 (Niflheim's overflow) among the dead the party failed.
- **No clean run exists.** Even a perfect, map-aided party loses someone. Act III's lesson,
  made personal: the party can be heroes and still not save everyone, and must live with the chalk.

## Reward (Return)
- **Mechanical:** survivors as Act IV allies; the Tally-Board; deep-gallery leveling; Ifrit's
  chamber scouted (eases the main quest's approach).
- **Narrative:** the evacuation's outcome sets the human stakes of the Ifrit fight and hands
  the party the visceral weight of triage before the finale asks them to triage *the world.*

## Throughline
- **Plants:** the named lost → Arc 18 (the dead the party failed); survivors → Arc 19 allies.
- **Pays off:** Wynne's ledger (Arc 1) — the book of crossed-out names, now in the party's hand;
  the Arc 9/14 cover-up cost, made of bodies.
- **"What are you becoming?":** the ones who decide which names stay on the board — the
  campaign's gentlest, hardest framing of its question.

## GM scripting notes
- Use a real, visible timer and a real list at the table. The emotional payload is the
  players *choosing* to cross a name out, not the GM narrating it.
- This is the dress rehearsal for the Arc 19 Choice — the same triage logic, scaled from a
  mine to a world. Make the players feel the practice.
