## Synopsis
Hugel should feel like a relief after Lighthalzen: air, water, sky, temple bells.
Then a Valkyrie descends during a public rite and kills someone she was meant to
bless. Priest Eadric asks the party to investigate because he cannot reconcile the
attack with his faith, and because he is honest enough not to explain it away.

The route through Manuk and El Dicastes outskirts into Abyss Lake turns the arc
from theology into battlefield evidence. Dragon pressure rises, temple wards are
inverted, and Zealot Bjorn gathers the frightened into a doctrine of surrender:
if heaven has turned on mortals, mortals must deserve it.

Valkyrie Randgris is the set-piece proof that the divine order is not merely angry.
It is destabilizing. Sigrun's dying words should give the party their clearest
pre-Arc-19 tell: there is a laugh behind the thrones, and the cascade is going
exactly the way that laugh intended.

## Spaces (the symbolic geography)
- **Threshold:** the temple stair at Odin's shrine — sacred ground that no longer welcomes the faithful.
- **Dream-landscape:** Abyss Lake — a drowned dragon-deep where even divine order has gone feral.
- **Climax:** the Valkyrie's hall, where heaven's own guardians attack the living.

## The Beats
1. Cold open: a Hugel festival of the gods; a Valkyrie descends — and kills.
2. Hook: the harvested feather from Arc 10; the temple's wards inverted.
3. Rising dungeon: Manuk/El Dicastes outskirts → Abyss Lake 1–3.
4. Turn: the gods aren't punishing the world — they're *coming apart,* same as the seals.
5. MVP: Valkyrie Randgris (+ Detardeurus).
6. Closing thread: a dying Valkyrie hears the Voice laugh (first explicit Loki tell).

## Throughline

- **Pays off:** the Voice seeded since Arc 1 — here it's almost confirmed divine.
- **"What are you becoming?":** if even gods unravel, what protects the party from the same end?
