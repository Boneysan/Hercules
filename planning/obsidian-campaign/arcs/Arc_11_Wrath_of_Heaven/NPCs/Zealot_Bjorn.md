# Zealot Bjorn

#custom #campaign #npc

> A Hugel hunter who lost his whole family to the first Valkyrie descent and concluded,
> in his grief, that they *deserved* it — that the gods' wrath is judgment, and the only
> survival is to be found worthy. Now he leads a flagellant band that marches *toward*
> the Valkyries, welcoming the slaughter as a sieve, and he has stopped being able to
> tell devotion from a death wish.

- **Role:** the arc's **human antagonist** — not a cultist, but a man the cascade *broke
  into* a villain. He obstructs the party by feeding the Valkyries the worshippers they kill.
- **Location:** the Hugel approach; the Odin temple steps, leading his band into the wards.
- **Propp function:** the villain who **blocks** (his flock bodily bars the temple, eager to
  be judged) and **traps** (he sabotages Eadric's calming rites, believing mercy is heresy).

## What he wants
To be found worthy by gods who have stopped sorting the worthy from anyone else. Bjorn needs
the wrath to *mean* something, because if the Valkyries are simply *malfunctioning* then his
family died for nothing — and that he cannot survive. His zeal is grief wearing armor.

## What he knows
- That the Valkyries don't discriminate anymore — though he refuses to believe it, the
  evidence is in every body his band leaves at the steps.
- The Whisperers tried to recruit him and he refused — "they want the gods *gone;* I want
  them *pleased*" — a useful datum that the cult and the zealots are *different* failures of
  the same crisis.
- Where the wards are weakest — knowledge he uses to lead his flock *in,* and the party could
  use to get through.

## Dialogue hooks (three doors)
1. *Compassion:* "Your family didn't die for a reason. That's the horror, and it's not your
   fault." → the hardest line in the arc; if the party reach the grief under the zeal (a real
   roleplay/Will contest), Bjorn *breaks down* and calls off his flock — saving dozens and
   clearing the temple steps without a fight.
2. *Force:* "Stand aside or be moved." → he welcomes martyrdom; the party must fight through
   true believers who *want* to die, an ugly, joyless battle that stains them.
3. *Exploit:* "Show us the weak wards, then." → he'll guide them in, assuming they march to
   be judged too; the party get the fast route at the cost of leading a death-march and
   confirming Bjorn's delusion. The Catechism notices using a grieving man as a key.

## Theme tie-in
Bjorn is what Eadric *could* become and refuses to: grief that curdles into the need for the
suffering to be *deserved.* He's Act III's intimate tragedy — the cascade doesn't only break
the world, it breaks the people in it into shapes that help it along. He asks the party
whether their own losses are making them harder or crueler.

## GM notes / secrets
- The *compassion* door is the arc's emotional set-piece — make talking Bjorn down as
  mechanically rewarding as a boss kill (clears the steps, saves his flock, frees Eadric's rite).
- If the party kill or exploit him, his flock's deaths are on the ledger; a survivor can recur
  in Arc 14 (Veins) as another grief-broken obstacle, showing the pattern spreading.
