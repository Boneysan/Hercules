## Synopsis
Lighthalzen is Act II's answer to every earlier compromise. The upper city is clean,
the slums are disposable, and the lift between them is the campaign's ugliest
threshold. Doctor Sabine Reuter asks the party for help because people are being
taken below and returned as bodies, specimens, or not at all.

The first jobs are medical and logistical: sample runs, Scaraba chitin orders,
registry checks. Then the language changes. The party find clone names, combat
templates, and performance notes that resemble their own victories. Rekenber has
not merely exploited the cascade; it has used the crisis to manufacture heroes at
scale.

Kiel D-01 and the Bio-Lab false heroes are the party's dark mirror. They are strong,
famous, optimized, and empty of consent. Echo's refusal keeps the arc from becoming
pure despair: one made thing says no. If the party protect that refusal, it becomes
one of the most important ending arguments in Arc 19.

## Spaces (the symbolic geography)
- **Threshold:** the slum-gate / the lab's hidden lift — descending from a city that
  pretends its underclass doesn't exist.
- **Dream-landscape:** the Bio Labs — a factory of *selves,* corridors of cloned
  champions; the trial space is the theme made architecture.
- **Climax:** Kiel D-01's core — a manufactured hero that knows it's manufactured.

## The Beats
1. Cold open: Lighthalzen's gleaming upper city; a body dumped from the slum wall.
2. Hook: Rekenber's clearance (Arc 7) opens the lab; Voss reappears as junior staff (if spared, Arc 2).
3. Rising dungeon: Scaraba Hole → Bio Lab F2 → F4.
4. Turn: the false heroes are *built from real templates* — and the party recognize the recipe (Cassell's, Voss's, their own).
5. MVP: Kiel D-01 (+ false heroes).
6. Closing thread: the demon coalition stirs (→ Arc 13); the gods begin to fail (→ Arc 11).
