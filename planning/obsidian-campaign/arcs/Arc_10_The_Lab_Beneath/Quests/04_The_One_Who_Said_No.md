# Quest — The One Who Said No

#custom #campaign #quest

> One clone got out. It's hiding in the slums, wearing a dead champion's face, and it
> wants exactly one thing the factory was built to prevent: to *not* be a hero. To not
> fight. To be left alone to be a person. The lab wants it back. Reise calls it a recall.

- **Type:** side — the arc's beating heart; the human (made) cost of the factory
- **Level band:** 120–150
- **Quest-giver:** [[../NPCs/Doctor_Sabine_Reuter]]
- **Key NPCs:** **Echo** (the escaped clone); [[../NPCs/Director_Hallan_Reise]]

## Hook (Separation)
Sabine is hiding a patient who shouldn't exist: a Bio-Lab clone, designation **Echo**, who
woke up *with* a conscience the deletion missed and walked out. It doesn't want to be
rescued into another war. It wants to be nobody. The threshold is moral: the party have
spent the campaign *becoming* stronger fighters — and here is a manufactured fighter
begging for the right to stop. To help it is to question their own trajectory.

## Spaces
- **Initiation:** the Lighthalzen slums — a maze of the deniable poor, where a person can
  hide precisely because the city refuses to look.
- **Reveal space:** a slum safehouse, then the recall-route the lab's retrieval units sweep —
  the factory reaching up into the city to take its property back.

## Challenge (conditions → consequences)
- **Hide or run Echo:** the party choose to conceal Echo in the slums (needs Sabine's
  network — her *compassion* door) or smuggle it out of the city (a chase against retrieval
  units). Company-friendly Arc 7 reputation makes the slums less willing to shelter it.
- **Echo won't fight:** mechanically, Echo *refuses combat* — the party can't solve its
  problem by making it a weapon. Any "just train it up" approach fails, on purpose. The
  quest resists the party's reflex.
- **Reise's recall:** Reise frames it as reclaiming an asset, not hunting a person — and
  offers the party a *bounty* to return it. Taking the bounty is a real, tempting fork.

## Quest item / Object (symbolic)
**Echo's Blank Tag** — the lab ID disc every clone wears, but Echo has *scratched out the
designation* and left it empty. A person insisting on being un-catalogued. It's the inverse
of Arc 10's Template Vial: the Vial reduces a person to a recipe; the Blank Tag is a
manufactured thing *refusing the recipe.* Held together, they're the whole question of the
campaign in two pieces of metal — *are you a pattern, or are you the one who said no?*

## Choices & branches (a price)
- **Protect Echo's right to be no one** → the party defend a "thing's" personhood at real
  cost (Reise's enmity, a recall squad, sheltering an "illegal asset") — and earn a quiet,
  unglamorous ally who reappears in Arc 18/19 as living proof a manufactured being can
  choose *not* to become a monster. The hopeful counter to Himmelmez.
- **Return Echo for the bounty** → easy coin and Reise's goodwill — and the party hand a
  person who begged to stop back into the factory. The Catechism tally takes its ugliest
  single hit; Echo's face *will* return in the Arc 19 mirror, accusing.

## Reward (Return)
- **Mechanical:** slum leveling; the Blank Tag; Echo as a (non-combat) recurring ally if saved.
- **Narrative:** the factory's victims given a single, specific face — and the party's
  "what are you becoming?" arc handed its most direct, *answerable* choice before Act III.

## Throughline
- **Plants:** Echo as the hopeful mirror → Arc 18 (Himmelmez's dark mirror), Arc 19 (the Choice — proof one *can* refuse the road).
- **Pays off:** the manufactured-self horror (Arcs 7, 9); the Catechism tally, at its most personal.
- **"What are you becoming?":** a made-thing asks to *not* become a weapon — and the party answer with their treatment of it.

## GM scripting notes
- Echo's refusal to fight is the design — never let the party "solve" it with violence or
  power-leveling. The quest's whole point is a problem swords don't touch.
- Keep Echo alive across the campaign if saved; an un-monstered clone standing beside the
  party at the Arc 19 Choice is one of the finale's strongest available images.
