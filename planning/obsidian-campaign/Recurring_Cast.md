## Brother Cassell — "The Gray Pilgrim" (recurring villain-foil, Acts I–III)

#custom #campaign #npc

> A travelling almsman in road-gray robes who always seems to have arrived in
> town just before things went wrong. Soft-spoken, generous to refugees, quotes
> scripture from three different faiths. He is the **Whisperers' field-shepherd** —
> the man who loosens the seals one town at a time.

> **Read-Aloud: Meeting Brother Cassell**
> > "The man tending to the wounded refugee wears simple, dust-stained gray robes. He has a warm, almost impossibly calm smile. He finishes wrapping the bandage, mutters a quick prayer to Odin, and stands up to face you. 'It is a dark time to be walking the roads,' he says, his voice smooth and comforting like warm honey. 'I am Brother Cassell. Are you the ones sent to stop the bleeding, or the ones causing it?'"

- **Propp function:** the villain who **blocks** and **traps** — rarely fights the
  party himself. He arranges the symptom, then is gone.
- **First appearance:** Arc 1, as the hooded figure who escapes the Prontera
  Culvert cell. The party only see his back and a goat-headed sigil ring.
- **Recurs:** Arc 4 (unmasked inside the Geffen Mages' Guild), Arc 9 (confirmed
  operating inside the Arunafeltz clergy), exits the stage in Act III when the
  gods and demons take over as antagonists.
- **What he wants:** not a single demon freed — *every seal weakened at once,* so
  the cascade becomes unstoppable. He believes he is performing a mercy: "The
  world is a wound that has been bandaged too tight. I only loosen the dressing."
- **What he knows:** the location of every seal-site the campaign visits, the
  identity of the next town's symptom, and — eventually — that he answers to
  [[Characters/Gods/Loki]] without knowing it is a god.
- **Dialogue hooks (always three doors):**
  1. *Noble:* "You're killing these people." → "I am letting them wake up."
  2. *Pragmatic:* "Who pays you?" → "A voice. It pays in certainty." (a Loki thread)
  3. *Dark:* "Teach me how the seals work." → he *will*, and it costs the party
     something — this is a real fork (see Arc 4).
- **Theme tie-in:** Cassell is what a *kind* person becomes when they decide the
  ends justify the unmaking. He is the party's "what are you becoming?" worn as a
  gentle smile.
- **Secret / GM:** Cassell is sincere and *not* the mastermind. Killing him early
  is allowed — but the cascade continues without him, which teaches the party the
  disease is bigger than any one cultist. Save that gut-punch.

## Quartermaster Wynne — Adventurers' Guild handler (recurring ally, Acts I–II)

#custom #campaign #npc

> A retired Prontera knight who lost a leg and now runs the guild contract desk at
> the fountain. Gruff, fair, keeps a ledger of every adventurer she's signed —
> and a separate one of the names she's had to cross out.

> **Read-Aloud: Meeting Quartermaster Wynne**
> > "The woman behind the Guild desk doesn't look up as you approach. She's busy crossing out a name in a thick, leather-bound ledger with a heavy sigh. She wears the battered chest plate of a Prontera Knight over civilian clothes, and a polished wooden peg leg rests against the desk. 'Don't just stand there breathing my air,' she barks, finally looking up with sharp, calculating eyes. 'You want to get rich, or do you want to die? Because the contract you're looking at guarantees one and risks the other.'"

- **Role:** the campaign's **quest-giver of record** and early mentor. She hands
  out the first guild contracts (framing Eden Group / Bounty Board kills) and is
  the party's link to the wider world as they travel.
- **What she knows:** rumors from every town's guild chapter — she is how the
  party *hear* about the next arc's symptom.
- **Arc of her own:** she can be **lost.** If the GM wants Act II to bite, the
  cult's reach reaches *her* — Wynne is a candidate for the ally the party fail to
  save, making the cascade personal. Her ledger becomes a quest object.
- **Dialogue hooks:** she never sugar-coats a contract. "It pays. It's also a bad
  idea. Both things are true. Sign here."
- **Theme tie-in:** Wynne *stopped* adventuring before it unmade her. She is the
  counter-example to Thanatos — proof you can put the sword down.

## The Voice (Loki, unseen until Act IV)

#custom #campaign #god

> Not an NPC the party meet — a *presence.* The intelligence behind the
> Whisperers. Cassell hears it as certainty; cultists hear it as scripture; a
> dying Valkyrie in Arc 11 hears it laugh.

- **Role:** the true architect. See [[Characters/Gods/Loki]]. Keep him entirely
  off-page through Acts I–III; he is only ever an *absence shaped like a plan.*
- **Reveal:** Arc 19. Until then, every "Voice" line the party hear should be
  deniable — could be madness, could be a god. Don't confirm.
- **Throughline:** seed one "Voice" moment per act (a cultist's last words, a
  scrawled note, a Valkyrie's laugh) so the Arc 19 reveal feels *earned,* not
  introduced.

## Mira Ashkey — Morroc refugee / keeper of the house-key (recurring witness, Acts I-IV)

#custom #campaign #npc

> A Morroc refugee first seen at Prontera's south gate in Arc 1. At low levels she is just one frightened survivor in the line for soup. By Arc 19, if the party kept paying attention to people instead of only bosses, she is the face that makes Morroc's destruction personal.

> **Read-Aloud: Meeting Mira Ashkey**
> > "Huddled near the soup kitchen is a young woman wrapped in a shawl that was once brightly colored, now caked in desert dust. She clutches something tightly against her chest—a brass house key, wrapped carefully in a frayed red thread. She watches the heavily armed knights pass with a mixture of hope and deep, exhausted terror. When she sees you looking, she flinches, hiding the key."

- **First appearance:** Arc 1, during [[Act_I_World_at_Peace/Arc_01_Omens_at_the_Fountain/Quests/04_The_Trembling_Ground]].
- **Recurs:** Arc 3 (Morroc rumors), Arc 5 (refugee ships), Arc 16 (Prontera evacuation rosters), Arc 19 (the ruined house-key).
- **Role:** witness / emotional ledger. She does not solve fights; she proves what the fights were for.
- **What she carries:** a brass house-key from Morroc, wrapped in red thread. It becomes the **Refugees' Key** in [[Act_IV_Ragnarok/Arc_19_Nightmare_of_Midgard/Quests/04_Where_Thanatos_Stood]].
- **Choice pressure:** if the party repeatedly protected refugees and civilians, Mira reaches Arc 19 alive and names the people who survived because of them. If not, the key is found without her.

## Echo — The One Who Said No (recurring ally / mirror, Acts II-IV)

#custom #campaign #npc

> A Bio Lab-made "hero" who refused the script Rekenber wrote into their body. Echo is living proof that being made for violence does not require becoming violence.

> **Read-Aloud: Meeting Echo**
> > "The figure standing over the defeated Rekenber guards lowers their glowing weapon. Their skin is pale, marked with strange alchemical burns and a barcode stamped onto their neck. They turn to you, and their eyes are a chilling, unnatural silver—the eyes of an engineered killer. But when they speak, their voice is trembling. 'They told me to kill you,' Echo whispers, dropping the weapon with a clang. 'I'm... I'm choosing not to.' They look at their own hands as if seeing them for the first time."

- **First appearance:** Arc 10, [[Act_II_The_Cracks/Arc_10_The_Lab_Beneath/Quests/04_The_One_Who_Said_No]].
- **Recurs:** Arc 18 (as a possible familiar dead / living contradiction to Himmelmez), Arc 19 (standing at the Choice if saved).
- **Role:** hopeful mirror. Thanatos shows what lonely heroism costs; Himmelmez shows what power hunger becomes; Echo shows refusal.
- **What they know:** the Bio Lab's conditioning methods, the terror of being designed as a tool, and the exact feeling of choosing otherwise.
- **Choice pressure:** if Echo lives and trusts the party, the Arc 19 Choice gains its clearest argument against Loki: made things can refuse their makers.
